# Vehicle Maintenance Guidance

## Purpose

Service and maintenance records provide evidence about the recorded
maintenance history of a vehicle. They should be interpreted using service
dates, odometer readings, service type, service center, description, source,
and provenance.

## Maintenance History

A useful maintenance history may contain:

- service dates
- recorded odometer readings
- service types
- service-center information
- repair or maintenance descriptions
- supporting invoices or records

A sequence of service records can help establish whether maintenance activity
is documented over time.

The absence of a service record does not by itself establish that maintenance
was not performed.

## Service Continuity

A consistent service history generally contains records whose dates and
odometer readings form a plausible chronological sequence.

For example:

- service at 20,000 km
- service at 35,000 km
- service at 50,000 km
- service at 65,000 km

Such records may provide supporting evidence of continued vehicle use and
maintenance.

The available records alone may not establish that every required maintenance
operation was completed.

## Maintenance Gaps

A significant gap between available service records may require additional
review.

Possible reasons for a gap include:

- maintenance performed outside the available service network,
- missing records,
- incomplete data,
- changes in service provider,
- or maintenance not being documented in the supplied dataset.

A gap should therefore be treated as missing evidence rather than automatic
proof that maintenance was neglected.

## Odometer Cross-Checks

Service records can provide useful mileage evidence.

When a service record contains an odometer value that conflicts with other
chronological mileage observations, the discrepancy should be preserved as an
evidence issue.

For example:

- service record: 60,000 km
- later record: 48,000 km

A decrease requires verification against additional records.

Service records can therefore contribute to an odometer verification process,
but they should not independently establish the cause of a mileage
inconsistency.

## Verification Actions

When maintenance history requires verification, review available evidence such
as:

- service invoices,
- authorized service-center records,
- independent workshop records,
- repair orders,
- inspection reports,
- dated photographs,
- odometer records,
- manufacturer maintenance records.

The purpose is to establish the most reliable chronological maintenance
history available.

## AI Grounding Rule

The AI must distinguish between:

1. documented maintenance activity,
2. missing maintenance evidence,
3. conflicting maintenance or mileage records,
4. and a confirmed maintenance condition.

The AI must not claim that required maintenance was skipped unless the supplied
evidence explicitly establishes that conclusion.

The absence of a record should be described as missing evidence rather than
proof that the maintenance did not occur.

## Vehicle-Specific Interpretation

Vehicle-specific service dates, odometer values, service centers, service
types, and maintenance descriptions must come from the vehicle evidence
supplied to the intelligence system.

This document provides general maintenance interpretation guidance only. It
must not be used as a source of vehicle-specific service facts.
