# Odometer Verification Guidance

## Purpose

Odometer records provide evidence about the reported mileage of a vehicle
over time. A reliable mileage history should generally show a logical
progression as the vehicle is used.

## Normal Mileage Progression

An odometer history normally increases over time.

For example:

- 20,000 km
- 35,000 km
- 51,000 km
- 68,000 km

An increase between observations is generally consistent with continued
vehicle use.

## Odometer Inconsistency

A decrease between two chronological odometer observations is an
inconsistency that requires investigation.

For example:

- 42,100 km
- 67,300 km
- 52,100 km

The decrease from 67,300 km to 52,100 km should not be treated as a normal
mileage progression.

The available records alone do not establish the reason for the decrease.
Possible explanations may include a data-entry error, a replacement
instrument cluster, a source-system issue, or inaccurate mileage reporting.

## Verification Actions

When an odometer inconsistency is identified, review additional evidence
such as:

- service invoices
- service-center records
- inspection records
- previous vehicle-history reports
- insurance claim records
- manufacturer service records
- photographs or other dated mileage evidence

The purpose of these checks is to establish the most reliable chronological
mileage history available.

## AI Grounding Rule

An odometer inconsistency is evidence requiring verification.

The AI must not state that mileage fraud or odometer tampering occurred unless
the available evidence explicitly establishes that conclusion.

The AI should distinguish between:

1. an observed mileage inconsistency,
2. a possible explanation,
3. and a confirmed finding.

## Vehicle-Specific Interpretation

Vehicle-specific mileage values must come from the vehicle evidence supplied
to the intelligence system.

This document provides general interpretation guidance only. It must not be
used as a source of vehicle-specific mileage facts.
